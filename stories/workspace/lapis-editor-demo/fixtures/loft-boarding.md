## Important Tips for Your Project

- Check the screws: Budget 12-packs usually do not include screws. You will need to grab a box of standard 4.0mm x 30mm multi-purpose wood screws to secure them.
- Calculate your spacing: On standard 400mm centered joists, you will typically need roughly 4 legs per square metre of boarding, but remember that rows overlap, so it is always safer to over-order slightly. [4]

## 1. How to Choose the Right Size (Height)

The height of your loft stilts depends entirely on your insulation thickness.

- Measure your insulation: Use a tape measure to check the current depth of your insulation. UK building regulations recommend a total depth of 270mm. [3]
- Measure your wood joists: Check the height of your existing timber joists (usually around 100mm).
- Calculate the required stilt height: Subtract your joist height from your total insulation height.
    
    - _Example:_ If you want 270mm of insulation and your joists are 100mm, you need 170mm of clearance. You should buy standard 175mm Loft Legs.
    - _Rule of thumb:_ Standard 175mm stilts work perfectly for almost all standard UK homes aiming for the 270mm insulation target.
    

## 2. How to Calculate How Many You Need

The exact number depends on the spacing of your wooden joists. Measure from the center of one joist to the center of the next. It will almost always be 400mm or 600mm.

## For 400mm Joist Spacing

- You need 8 stilts per square metre for the main body of the floor.
- Add 2 extra stilts per linear metre for the perimeter edges where rows start and stop.
- _Quick Formula:_ $\text{Total Area (m}^2\text{)} \times 8 + \text{Perimeter allowance}$

## For 600mm Joist Spacing

- You need 5 stilts per square metre for the main body.
- Add 1.5 extra stilts per linear metre for the edges.
- _Quick Formula:_ $\text{Total Area (m}^2\text{)} \times 5 + \text{Perimeter allowance}$

## Example Calculation (4m x 3m Room = 12m²) on 400mm Joists

- Base amount: $12 \times 8 = 96 \text{ stilts}$.
- Edge allowance: Add roughly 10% to 15% more for staggered joints and ends.
- Total to buy: Around 110 stilts (which equals about 9 packs of 12). [4]

## 3. What Goes on Top of the Stilts?

You must use Tongue and Groove (T&G) Loft Boarding chipboards. Regular sheets of plywood or standard chipboard are too large to fit through a standard loft hatch and are difficult to lock together safely over stilts.

## The Best Materials to Buy

- 18mm Tongue & Groove Chipboard: This is the UK industry standard. It is strong, budget-friendly, and locks together on all four sides to distribute weight evenly.
- Size to look for: Look for 320mm x 1220mm panels. These are specifically manufactured to fit perfectly through narrow loft hatches and align across standard joist spacings. [5]

## How to calculate the boards you need

- Each standard 320mm x 1220mm board covers exactly $0.39\text{m}^2$.
- To find your total boards: Divide your total square metres by 0.39, then add 10% for cutting waste.
- _Example for 12m²:_ $12 \div 0.39 = 31 \text{ boards}$. With 10% waste, you should buy 34 boards.
